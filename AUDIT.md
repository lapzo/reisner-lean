## Mathematical choices and reference verification

There is **no change to the requested criterion** and no added purity,
infinite-field, or nonempty-vertex hypothesis. Variables have degree one;
Panov uses degree two, which does not affect the ring property. Degrees of
reduced homology are integers, and the complex consisting of the empty face
has dimension −1 and reduced homology `k` in degree −1.

The formalized monomial theorem has coefficient hypotheses
`[CommSemiring k] [Nontrivial k]` and arbitrary ambient `V`. Polynomial
membership now holds over every `[CommSemiring k]`, including the trivial
case. The actual quotient, its standard monomial basis, and scalar-map
injectivity are checked over `[CommRing k]`; quotient nontriviality needs
`[Nontrivial k]`. All work with arbitrary ambient `V`, without purity or
additional complex hypotheses. The ultimate target remains over a field
and finite ambient vertices.

The complete proof route is:

1. Nonface generators → monomial membership → quotient monomial basis.
2. Coordinate-prime quotients → Krull dimension `d = max |F|`.
3. The graded local test and algebraic depth → local-cohomology vanishing below `d`.
4. Localized monomial pieces → graded Cech complex → link cohomology with shift `q − |F| − 1`.
5. Field duality converts cohomology vanishing to homology vanishing.
6. Linkwise vanishing forces purity; this converts the local link-dimension bound to the uniform `d − |F| − 1` bound and proves the converse.

## Quotient-grading proof implementation

For `[CommRing R]`, `weightedFaceRingComponent` maps the finitely supported
coordinate functions whose face-exponent indices lie in one weight fiber
through the inverse coordinate equivalence of `faceRingBasis`. Its span
theorem identifies that submodule with the span of the corresponding basis
vectors. `weightedFaceRing_isInternal` proves that fibers are independent and
their supremum is top: basis coordinates are partitioned by their unique
weighted degree. This uses `LinearMap.iSupIndep_map`,
`Finsupp.disjoint_supported_supported`, and the union of all degree fibers.

`weightedFaceRingComponent_eq_polynomial_image` equates the coordinate fiber
with the image of `MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousSubmodule` under the
actual ideal-quotient map. It rewrites the weighted homogeneous submodule as
the span of its monomials, then uses `faceRingBasis_apply`; nonface monomials
vanish by ideal membership. `weightedFaceRingComponent_mul` transfers
`MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousSubmodule_mul` through that image equality.
These results package as the actual `GradedAlgebra` on `K.FaceRing R`.

`weight_fine_eq` specializes the general fibers to exponent-indexed fine
degree, while `weight_one_eq_sum` specializes to exponent-sum total degree.
The fine components are monomial spans (or bottom for nonfaces); total
components are standard-basis spans and equal the supremum of the fine
components of the same total degree. For degree zero, both components are
`Submodule.span R {1}`. `faceRingDegreeZeroSubalgebra` uses the total grading;
`faceRingDegreeZeroEquiv` restricts the scalar algebra map and proves it
bijective using the already checked scalar injectivity and the degree-zero
span. `faceRingDegreeZeroEquiv_apply` identifies its value with the scalar
image. All constructions preserve the empty face, ghost vertices, arbitrary
ambient types, and trivial commutative coefficient rings.

## Previous coordinate-prime and facet milestone

The checked `prop:facet-primes` links 19 compiled declarations. `coordinateIdeal`
is the span of variables outside an arbitrary finite `F`.
`mem_coordinateIdeal_iff` characterizes membership term by term over a
commutative semiring. Over a commutative ring, `coordinateRestriction` uses
`MvPolynomial.killCompl`; its kernel is `coordinateIdeal`, it is surjective,
and `coordinateQuotientEquiv` identifies the actual quotient with the
polynomial ring on the subtype of vertices in `F`. The inside/outside variable
formulas are checked. The ideal is prime over domains. Over fields,

## Localization-kernel audit and regressions

The polynomial-fraction theorem is valid for all commutative coefficient
rings, including the zero ring. The localization criterion supplies a witness
`n ≥ 0` with `x_F^n * p = 0`. For `n > 0`, monomial multiplication maps each
exponent to `α + n • 1_F`; the translation is injective and preserves each
coefficient, so terms cannot cancel. Its support is exactly
`F ∪ support α`. The termwise Stanley–Reisner criterion therefore makes one
factor sufficient. A witness `n=0` means the numerator class is already zero.
This special conclusion uses squarefree support membership and is not a general
property of localization. For example, in `k[x,y]/(x²y)`, localizing at `x`
kills `y` by `x²` but `xy` is nonzero.
